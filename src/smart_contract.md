# Smart Contract

The smart contract plays an important role in the RLN protocol. Having it may confuse you, as you might think that the RLN protocol should be used on-chain. However, the registry and slashing are the only mechanisms that happen on-chain. The actual usage of RLN, after a user registers, is off-chain, and if they break the rate limit they will be slashed and removed from the registry on-chain.

As mentioned, the smart contract only plays a registry role in the protocol. Though it's not necessary to use an on-chain smart-contract registry, it's good practice, because the best fit for RLN is *non-consensus anonymous environments*, and the only thing that should be truly/verifiably consistent is a user set. That's why, to store it, we need a registry, preferably on a blockchain.

You can find a [Foundry project for the RLN registry](https://github.com/Rate-Limiting-Nullifier/rln-contract) on our GitHub. It was audited internally by PSE.

The project contains:
* **RLN.sol** - main contract; functionality for registration and withdrawal (or slashing);
* **Verifier.sol** & **IVerifier.sol** - generated contracts for zkSNARK (Groth16) verification used for withdrawal (or slashing).

We'll focus on the main components of the registry contract and skip the verifier, because it was generated by SnarkJS.

## Registry contract

### Constructor

The constructor gets all the necessary parameters for initialization of the contract. These parameters are immutable, so it's not possible to change them after initialization.

It takes the following parameters:
* minimalDeposit: minimal membership deposit, cost of 1 message;
* maximalRate: maximal rate - maximum number of messages one can send;
* depth: depth of the Merkle tree;
* feePercentage: fee percentage;
* feeReceiver: address of the fee receiver;
* freezePeriod: amount of blocks for withdrawal time-lock;
* _token: address of the ERC20 contract;
* _verifier: address of the Groth16 Verifier.

Fees play an important role in the economic side of the protocol. They prevent the self-slashing attack vector by making it economically inefficient; and fees also prevent money laundering schemes.

### Registration

The **register** function is used for registration. It receives \\(identityCommitment\\) as an argument as well as the stake amount. Then it calculates messageLimit based on the stake amount:
\\[messageLimit = amount / MINIMAL\\_DEPOSIT\\]. 

Then it stores the \\(identityCommitment\\) in the users' set and emits an event. Client nodes need to listen to the event, because based on that they calculate the \\(rateCommitment\\) value. It's an important part, because by doing that we don't need to calculate the Poseidon hash on-chain, which is not cheap.

It's also important to understand that we may not want to have different rate limits for different users. We can enforce that by setting a fixed stake amount.

### Withdraw | Slashing

There are two terms: *withdraw* and *slash* used in the RLN protocol. Let's define them:
* **withdraw** - when the account that withdraws an \\(identityCommitment\\) (and stake) is the same that registered it;
* **slash** - when the account that withdraws an \\(identityCommitment\\) (and stake) is different from the one that registered it.

It's important to separate these terms, because in the withdraw method, users don't have to pay fees. The reason is that if it's the same account that's used for withdrawal, then it's clearly not money laundering. That's not the case with slashing, and that's why users pay a fee for slashing.

Self slashing is not a problem, because users will still lose some amount of their stake, so it's economically inefficient.

But there was a problem with *self withdrawal with front-run*. It's when users spam and quickly remove/withdraw themselves before others can do it. The problem is solved with time-lock withdrawal: when users initialize the withdraw, their funds are locked for **freezePeriod** blocks, giving others an opportunity to slash (slashing happens immediately).

The withdraw function emits an event, and it's important for client nodes to listen to it, as they should remove the withdrawn \\(identityCommitment\\) from their membership Merkle tree. If they don't, the removed user will be able to spam.

## Important notes

You may have a question: who is the fee receiver? In general, that's an application-level question, because the creator of an RLN app may want to receive fees or donate them.

We recommend burning the fees.

But it's important to understand: burning centralized ERC20 stablecoins such as USDT or USDC (sending to the zero address or calling the burn function in the token contract) is synonymous with transferring money to those companies. What we recommend is creating another contract that will be the fee receiver. The tokens will be locked in the contract, and it'll contain only one function - burn.
Anyone can call this burn function, and it'll swap locked tokens to ETH on any DEX, pay the caller's gas, and burn the swapped ETH.
